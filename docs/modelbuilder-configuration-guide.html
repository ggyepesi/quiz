ModelBuilder Configuration Guide

What a model can say about a domain built on Wikidata, and how the checked-in domains use each option. The examples come from the model files under data/wikidata/; labels in italics are the editor's own wording.

Projects, models and imports

A project is either a Domain, a servable quiz product, or a Model, a reusable library of class declarations such as Person, Astronomy, Discovery or Historical Positions. A Model can still generate and save its own local instances.

Imports are live references to another project's class configurations and selections, never copies of its complete generated snapshot. An imported class declaration is read-only schema, and the owner's class snapshot stays in the owning model. A referenced population selection is the explicit publication boundary. An ordinary population contributes exactly its saved QIDs and class. When that class has a materialized transformation source, the class owns one separately saved typed object graph; the referenced selection contributes only its published members from that graph, so transformed fields and links survive without exposing the owner's other instances. Unreferenced populations and imported classes contribute no instances. History uses PositionWithHoldersPopulation this way; PositionWithHolders supplies its schema and published materialized members, not the Historical Positions snapshot. An edit to Person reaches every importer the next time it loads, with nothing re-imported. An importer builds on imported classes with its own classes, subclasses and represented-as rules.

The workflow is always the same: configure, Generate (Wikidata acquisition into one object graph), show and curate, then Save model/domain, which is the only point where anything is written. A saved domain is what the quiz and the web client serve. A generation depth sets how far references are followed from the root class.

Why these constructs

Wikidata is a graph of statements. Each statement is a triple:

subjectan item ── property ──▶ objectan item or a value

The subject is always an entity: an item with a QID such as Q42, or a property with a PID. The object is either another item or a plain value such as a string, a date, a quantity or a media file. An entity carries nothing but its labels, descriptions, aliases, sitelinks and the statements made about it.

A statement is not a bare triple. It may carry qualifiers, which are property–value pairs that give it context: a nomination's for work, an office's start time and end time, a prize's together with. It also carries references and a rank. A statement has no QID of its own, and no other statement can point at it.

Each ModelBuilder construct maps one part of that structure to a class:

ConstructAn instance representsIdentified by
Source classA Wikidata entity: a person, a constellation, a positionIts QID
Statement classOne Wikidata statement, a triple together with its qualifiers: a nomination, an office holdingA content key over its fields, because the statement has no QID
Owned classA part of one entity grouped into an object of its own: a person's structured name, a body's discoveryThe owner's QID and the field that produced it
Aggregate classA group of statements or entities that Wikidata does not model as one thing: a Nobel Prize year and category with all its laureatesThe values of its key fields
Graph classA decision about an entity reached by walking statements across the graph: whether a position belongs in the domainThe entity it classifies and the graph class

The owned, aggregate and graph classes have no direct counterpart in Wikidata. Owned and aggregate classes shape data that already exists into objects a quiz can use. A graph class decides which entities belong in the domain at all.

Fields follow the same structure. On a source class, an outgoing field reads the object of a statement whose subject is the instance; an incoming field reads the subject of a statement whose object is the instance. On a statement class, fields hold the subject, the object and the statement's qualifiers. A field can also restrict which statements it reads by a qualifier's value, or come from another source altogether: a Wikipedia infobox, DBpedia, a sitelink count or an incoming-link count. Derived fields such as inverts, unions and companion matches are computed from what has already been loaded.

What Wikidata does not have

Some configuration constructs have no Wikidata counterpart, because they answer questions Wikidata leaves to its users.

  • Owned class: Wikidata has no notion of a part of an item. A person's given and family names are two separate statements; an owned Name groups them into one object.
  • Aggregate class: Wikidata has no item for "the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics" with its three laureates. An aggregate builds it from the statements.
  • Graph class: deciding which entities belong in a domain is a modelling decision. A graph walks statements to make it and records the evidence.
  • Vocabulary and population selections: a named set of items is configuration, not data. Wikidata has class items, but no saved list of chosen items.
  • Derived fields (invert, union): Wikidata stores each statement once, on its subject. Reading it from the object's side is either an incoming field, which queries Wikidata, or an invert, which reuses what has already been loaded.

Item or statement?

Whether Wikidata records a real-world thing as an item or keeps it as a statement on another item decides which construct models it here. Wikidata has no single directive for this. Three things govern it.

Notability decides whether an item may exist

Wikidata:Notability admits an item that meets any one of three criteria: a sitelink to a Wikimedia project; a clearly identifiable entity described by serious public sources; or a structural need, meaning the item "is needed to make statements made in other items more useful". The structural-need criterion is the one that matters here, because it allows an item whose only purpose is to be the value of statements. How far it should reach has been debated for years (notability reform, notability overhaul).

The data model sets the technical limit

As Help:Statements describes, a statement can carry qualifiers, references and a rank, but it cannot be the value of another statement, and it cannot have sitelinks or statements of its own. When a fact must be pointed at by other facts, or needs more than its qualifiers can hold, it has to become an item. The qualifier P805 statement is subject of links a statement to such an item.

Conventions differ by subject area

  • Help:Modelling/Other domains recommends separate items for an institution and its building when their histories diverge.
  • WikiProject Performing arts creates an item for a premiere production whenever more is to be said about it.
  • The EveryPolitician data model keeps memberships as qualified P39 statements rather than items.
  • Data Quality Days 2022 argued for avoiding unnecessary items and folding over-specific ones back into a property and qualifiers.
  • WikiProject Ontology/Modelling collects these discussions.

In practice a relation stays a qualified statement until something else needs to point at it or it needs facts of its own; then it becomes an item, often an edition or event item such as a ceremony, a production or an election. That is a summary of the conventions, not a rule any Wikidata page states.

In these models: where Wikidata kept the relation as a statement, it is a statement class with a content key (Nomination on P1411, the Nobel P166 statements, OfficeHolding on P39). Where Wikidata promoted it to an item, it is an ordinary source class: Ceremony, reached through Nomination's P805 qualifier. Because the same kind of fact is sometimes an item and sometimes a statement, even within one subject area, check with Explore or Sample before choosing between the two.

The five class constructs

Every class shares one header: Class name, Alias and Extends. Each also has a declaration id that a rename does not move. What differs is how instances come into being.

Source class entities

How do we find instances of this class? Instances are Wikidata entities, identified by QID unless you choose otherwise.

  • Membership: by relation (P31 = Q8928 constellation, or a set of P31 values, optionally with subclasses through P279*), by any other property into a target set, by explicit seed QIDs, by a named selection, or unbounded when the class is filled by fields that point at it.
  • Population limits: Exclude instances of, Own population limit with Rank by, Require label in a language, and Notable only, which requires an English Wikipedia article.
  • Identity: the QID, or a content key over chosen fields, with a policy for when a key cannot be computed and how each field combines when the same key occurs twice.
  • Display name: the Wikidata label, a field, or an ordered list of fields to try in turn.
  • Represent matching entities as: reclassify each fetched instance by evidence after acquisition.
  • Extras: aliases as additional names, and an English Wikipedia source link.

Statement class content key

Instances are Wikidata statements promoted to objects, for example one Oscar nomination. A statement class configures exactly one triple:

subjectPerson ── P1411 nominated for ──▶ objectOscarCategories
  • Property: the PID that joins the two ends, such as P1411 or P39 position held.
  • Subject bound: restricts whose statements are collected. A subject population reads statements outward from subjects already extracted.
  • Object bound (allowed objects): restricts which statements are kept. It can be a list, a relation or a vocabulary.
  • Find subjects using objects from: a separate bound that finds which subjects to load without filtering the statements kept on them.
  • Bound one end or both. An open end does not by itself cause a Wikidata-wide scan: a bounded subject population can safely read all of its outgoing values. When both the entity set and the allowed value set are known, rule R16 pins both sets in the query to avoid a broad, non-deterministic intermediate join.
  • Identity: a content key over fields such as nominee, category and year, with a missing-qualifier policy and a policy for superseded duplicates.
  • Fields: Statement subject, Statement object, Statement participants (the subject plus entity qualifiers as one collection), qualifier fields such as P585 or P580/P582, and derived fields.

Owned class per owner

One component object per owner, created by a field on the owner: Person.structuredName → Name makes a Name on the person's QID and loads given and family names onto it.

  • Identity: class at its production site plus the owner's QID, so two fields that both target Name never collapse.
  • Every owned class has a derived, read-only Owner reference back to the instance that produced it.
  • One owned schema may have several production sites on different owner classes. Discovery is owned by both celestial bodies and constellations.

Aggregate class grouping

Groups the instances of another class by key fields: From class, Key fields and Aggregated fields. It is a real class with its own identity, so it can be served, searched and quizzed like any other.

Graph class traversal

A population discovered by walking relations and classified by evidence.

  • Start node: a class or a saved population.
  • Next nodes: an ordered path. Each step has a property, a direction and optional equivalent alternative edges. A step may name an admission population (Admit and expand).
  • Evidence: relations from a node and tests on what they reach, with a policy for incomplete evidence.
  • Result: All, Accepted, Review and Rejected tabs. Manual accept or reject is stored as a separate mark and never rewrites the original result.
  • Apply result: narrows the output class to the accepted population, or adds memberships to it. Repeat until no new instances are found computes a closure.

Derived fields cost no queries. A field whose target is a vocabulary name builds that vocabulary from the values it loads, so declaring the field is the discovery.

Inheritance, classification and representation

  • Extends: ordinary single inheritance. A subclass may have its own membership rule, and its members are then base instances too.
  • Subclass condition: classifies existing base instances locally, by a property value (a P31 value with its descendants), or by whether the QID is inside or outside a saved population.
  • Entity-kind rules: map P31 evidence to a modelled kind, for example Person when P31 = Q5.
  • Represented-as rules: turn a placeholder class into a concrete one per instance. A Laureate is represented as a Person when the evidence says so. An instance that fails the evidence keeps its placeholder class; it is neither dropped nor given a class it does not have.

Selections

A selection is a named set that other constructs refer to. It is never served by itself.

  • Vocabulary selection: a named set of QIDs, used either as allowed values (the value is a member) or as a tag set (the value's P31 is a member). One vocabulary can bound both a field and a statement's object position.
  • Population selection: one class plus the stable QIDs of chosen instances. You create it from loaded instances with Create population selection…, then reuse it as a graph start, an admission boundary, a subclass condition or a statement bound. It crosses imports.

Real domains, construct by construct

Constellations

importsextendsownedrepresented asrequired filter

Assembled from three models: Astronomy, Discovery and Person.

  • Constellation (source): P31 ∈ {Q8928, Q4193029}, excluding Q4158896. abbreviation (P1813) is required, so anything without an IAU abbreviation drops out. sharesBorderWith is a self-reference collection (P47).
  • stars and galaxies are related objects read incoming through P59 and sorted by apparentMagnitude.
  • Astronomy model: CelestialBody is the base; Star, BrightStar, NamedGalaxyStar and Galaxy extend it. Each concrete class declares its own apparent magnitude, and only BrightStar filters it (≤ 6.0, required). NamedGalaxyStar and Galaxy also require Notable only.
  • discovery is an owned component. The Discovery class holds date (P575) and discoverers (P61). Discoverer is represented as Person; the owner is represented as Constellation here and as CelestialBody in Astronomy.

Nobel Prizes

statementparticipantsaggregatevocabulary
  • LaureatesWithMotivation (statement): P166 award received, with allowed objects from the Categories vocabulary. category is the statement object, year is qualifier P585 (required) and motivation is qualifier P6208.
  • laureates is Statement participants: the subject plus qualifier P1706 together with, so a shared prize becomes one statement with several laureates.
  • NobelPrize (aggregate) groups those statements by category and year, so each prize lists all its laureates.
  • Laureate is a placeholder represented as the imported Person.

Oscar nominations

statementqualifierscompanion matchdescriptive vocabularies
  • Nomination (statement): P1411 nominated for, bounded by the OscarCategories vocabulary, with a limit of 20,000. Qualifiers give nominee (P2453), forWork (P1686) and ceremony (P805).
  • won is a companion match: true when a P166 award-received statement with the same value and role exists.
  • Nominee and ForWork have no membership rule of their own. They are exactly the entities those fields point at.
  • Their fields type (P31 into NomineeType) and genre (P136 into WorkGenre) build those two vocabularies as they load.
  • Ceremony is a real class with its own year. Nominee is represented as Person.

Historical Positions (model)

  • Position: P31 = Q114962596, limit 200,000. Counting fields use other sources: sitelinkCount, holderCount (incoming P39) and inheritedHolderCount.
  • GraphConstraint (graph) starts from PositionDiscoveryStart, walks P31 incoming and classifies Positions by evidence.
  • PositionReplacementExpansion (graph) follows P1365 replaces and P1366 replaced by repeatedly until nothing new is reached, and adds memberships to PositionWithHolders.
  • Curated results become the populations PositionPopulation and PositionWithHoldersPopulation.

History (domain)

  • Imports Position and PositionWithHolders.
  • OfficeHolding (P39 statement) finds its subjects through PositionWithHoldersPopulation but keeps every position those holders held, with start, end, predecessor and successor qualifiers.
  • ReachablePosition is Position outside that population. This subclass condition marks the boundary positions.
  • Person.offices inverts OfficeHolding, and spouse is a self-reference. PositionHolder is represented as Person.

The plain case: one source class (P31 = Q11344) with thirteen simple properties, from symbol and atomic number to melting point and discoverer. A complete quiz domain can be built from membership and fields alone.

Sketch: football careers

A design sketch. The PIDs and QIDs have not been checked against Wikidata.

  1. Import Person.
  2. Player (source): P106 = Q937857 association football player, notable only, limited and ranked by sitelink count.
  3. Spell (statement): Player · P54 member of sports team · Club, with Player as subject population. Qualifiers P580/P582 give dates and P1350/P1351 give matches and goals. Identity: player, club and start date.
  4. Club has no membership of its own. Its country field (P17) builds a ClubCountry vocabulary for faceting.
  5. Player.spells and Club.players are inverts of Spell.
  6. Optionally, a CareerSpan aggregate groups spells by player.
  7. For an exact national squad, curate a population selection of players and use it as the Spell statement's subject-discovery bound. Every club those players played for is kept.

Each step reuses a construct shown above, so a new domain is mostly a choice of membership, triple and production per field.

All 193 [[Member states of the United Nations|member states]] and 2 [[United Nations General Assembly observers|observer states]] of the [[United Nations]], in addition to several [[De facto state|''de facto'' states]], represent themselves with [[national flag]]s. National flags generally contain symbolism of their respective state and serve as an emblem which distinguishes themselves from other states in [[International relations|international politics]]. National flags are adopted by [[government]]s to strengthen national bonds and legitimate formal authority. Such flags may contain symbolic elements of their [[people]]s, [[Military|militaries]], [[Territory|territories]], [[Power (social and political)|rulers]], and [[Dynasty|dynasties]]. The [[flag of Denmark]] is the oldest flag still in current use as it has been recognized as a national symbol since the 14th century.

== Background and definitions ==
{{See also|History of flags}}
According to the ''[[Collins English Dictionary]]'', a [[national flag]] is "a [[flag]] that represents or is an emblem of a [[country]]."<ref>{{Cite encyclopedia |title=national flag |encyclopedia=[[Collins English Dictionary]] |publisher=HarperCollins |location=Glasgow |url=https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/national-flag |access-date=2024-08-18}}</ref> The word ''country'' can be used to refer to a [[sovereign state]], sometimes also called an independent state.<ref>{{cite web|last=Rosenberg |first=Matt |date=2024-08-18 |title=Differences Between a Country, State, and Nation |url=https://www.thoughtco.com/country-state-and-nation-1433559 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240802075841/https://www.thoughtco.com/country-state-and-nation-1433559 |archive-date=2024-08-02 |access-date=2024-08-18 |website=[[ThoughtCo]]}}</ref> It is [[Customary law|customary]] in [[international law]] that states adopt a flag to distinguish themselves from other states.<ref>{{Citation |last=Tóth |first=Zoltán |title=Constitutional and Legal Protection of State and National Symbols in Central Europe |chapter=The Protection of State and National Symbols Across Europe: An Overview of Constitutional Law and Criminal Law Regulations |series=Studies of the Central European Professors' Network |date=2022 |page=39 |chapter-url=https://real.mtak.hu/158070/ |access-date=2024-08-17 |place=Miskolc, Budapest |publisher=Central European Academic Publishing |language=en |doi=10.54237/profnet.2022.ztclpsnsce_2 |isbn=978-615-6474-18-6}}</ref>

National flags are considered to "provide perhaps the strongest, clearest statement of national identity," and governments have used them to promote and create bonds within the country, motivate [[patriotism]], honor the efforts of citizens, and [[Legitimacy (political)|legitimate formal authority]].<ref>{{cite journal|last=Cerulo |first=Karen A. |date=1993-06-01 |title=Symbols and the world system: National anthems and flags |url=https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01115492 |journal=Sociological Forum |language=en |volume=8 |issue=2 |page=244 |doi=10.1007/BF01115492 |issn=1573-7861}}</ref> Throughout history, elements within flags have been used to symbolize rulers, dynasties, [[Territory|territories]], [[Military|militaries]], and [[people]]s of their respective countries.<ref>{{cite book|last1=Eriksen |first1=Thomas Hylland |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=eT18AgAAQBAJ |title=Flag, Nation and Symbolism in Europe and America |last2=Jenkins |first2=Richard |date=2007-10-18 |publisher=Routledge |isbn=978-1-134-06695-7 |pages=334–350 |language=en}}</ref> Flags also conceptually represent a [[Value (ethics and social sciences)#Cultural values|country's core values]], such as [[In-group and out-group|group membership]] and love for the country.<ref>{{cite journal|last1=Becker |first1=Julia C. |last2=Butz |first2=David A. |last3=Sibley |first3=Chris G. |last4=Barlow |first4=Fiona Kate |last5=Bitacola |first5=Lisa M. |last6=Christ |first6=Oliver |last7=Khan |first7=Sammyh S. |last8=Leong |first8=Chan-Hoong |last9=Pehrson |first9=Samuel |last10=Srinivasan |first10=Narayanan |last11=Sulz |first11=Aline |last12=Tausch |first12=Nicole |last13=Urbanska |first13=Karolina |last14=Wright |first14=Steven C. |year=2017 |title=What Do National Flags Stand for? An Exploration of Associations Across 11 Countries |url=http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022022116687851 |journal=Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology |language=en |volume=48 |issue=3 |page=336 |doi=10.1177/0022022116687851 |issn=0022-0221|hdl=10023/9858 |hdl-access=free }}</ref> In 1975, American vexillologist [[Whitney Smith]] stated thus regarding the role of flags in society:{{Sfn|Znamierowski|2020|p=100}}<ref>{{cite book|last=Smith |first=Whitney |author-link=Whitney Smith |url=https://archive.org/details/flagsthroughages00smit/page/31/mode/2up |title=Flags through the ages and across the world |date=1975 |publisher=McGraw-Hill |isbn=978-0-07-059093-9 |location=New York |pages=32 |url-access=registration}}</ref><ref>{{Cite thesis |last=Brownell |first=Frederick Gordon |title=Convergence and Unification: the national flag of South Africa (1994) in historical perspective |date=2015 |access-date=2024-09-07 |degree=PhD |publisher=University of Pretoria |url=https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstream/handle/2263/50847/Brownell_Convergence_2015.pdf |page=23}}</ref>
